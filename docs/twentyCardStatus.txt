Twenty-card catalog - October 30, 2026

What this adds

There are twenty common U.S. consumer reward products in the existing C
catalog. There is no database server to install. Card data stays in realCards.c,
and the wallet saves the chosen IDs in the same small text format as before.
IDs 1-5 still mean the practice cards; IDs 101-105 keep their original real
product names. The new records continue through 120. The wallet holds five
selected card types, while the catalog has twenty choices.

The research files are twentyCardResearch.txt and cardTermsFollowUp.txt.
The follow-up fills more fee and benefit details and records source versions.
Each product has issuer sources
and a check date. This is not a verified ranking by number of cardholders.
Popularity surveys, website clicks and recommendations measure different
things, so the app calls these twenty common cards rather than claiming an
official top twenty.

Reading the cards

Run build\cardClarity.exe --gate4 --gui. Type part of a name above the list.
The search ignores letter case. Clear it to see all twenty products again.
If the search has no match, the app says the exact product may be missing
and disables Add. It does not choose a similar card for you. Searching does
not change the wallet or save file. A selected card shows its ordinary earning
unit, annual fee, costs, earning limits, benefit conditions, source gaps and
dated links. Scroll the notes to read them all. Long list names can scroll
sideways. The search stays separate from the saved-card list.

In the console, run build\cardClarity.exe --gate4. Choose 1 for the short
catalog, then enter one of its IDs to read that card's complete summary.
Choose 0 to return. Option 2 opens the existing wallet actions. Use its Add
option with a listed ID, save, then restart to check the same product returns.
The six purchase examples still use the original five real cards; follow
gateFourDemo.txt for those.

What the research checks

Known costs appear before benefit claims. First-year annual-fee waivers are
separate from the fee in later years. Some cards need a paid membership even
though the card's annual fee is zero. Benefit notes explain allowance periods,
required bookings, activation, spending caps, paid renewals and redemption
limits. Insurance needs qualifying events, payment and claims; it is not
guaranteed cash. No advertised annual benefit total is treated as profit.

The follow-up found dated Amex agreements, Chase fee schedules, Citi pricing
tables, Bank of America's sample agreement and Capital One disclosures.
The June 2026 Amex agreements replace March as the latest read here. Indexed
Citi offers and older tables sometimes disagree, so the app labels the
versions separately. Some live fields and three full Chase pricing links
remain unavailable. Missing numbers stay unconfirmed, never zero. Dated
agreements are not confirmed current application offers. These summaries
cover selected terms; the account's current contract and insurance policy
control.
This version does not update terms over the internet.

Comparison limits

The original five cashback cards keep their working purchase calculations.
Blue Cash Preferred adds modeled streaming, gas and transit rewards.
Eligible Lyft rides also receive transit's 3%. Its grocery result stays
a capped range because the form does not collect that
card's prior grocery use. Everyday's allowance is separate and is not reused.

Freedom Flex, Double Cash, Discover, Customized Cash, Prime Visa and Costco
need extra activation, payment, membership, merchant or shared-cap information
to calculate fairly. Their summaries are available, but the comparison says
needs review. The eight points/miles cards keep their actual reward units;
there is no guessed cash value. A wallet containing any card that needs
review cannot show an overall winner. Credits, welcome offers, annual fees,
interest and insurance payouts are not added to a purchase cashback figure.

Checks actually run

GCC C11 with -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Werror builds the app and C checks.
All 40 original module checks, 31 Gate 1 cases, 39 Gate 2 cases, 34 original
Gate 4 cases and 31 expanded-catalog cases passed on Linux. The new suite
opens the actual console for every product, reads its costs and earning
notes, adds and saves it, starts another process, checks the reloaded ID,
and removes it. Other cases check fee periods, reward units, missing searches,
unsupported comparisons, separate Amex caps and a damaged later saved ID.
The added rideshare case runs the actual menu and checks $3 on a $100 ride.
These software checks verify output and behavior; they do not prove that a
bank's future offer or an individual insurance claim will match the summary.
The actual output is in gateFourBuildLog.txt and gateFourTestLog.txt.

Clang 19 AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer builds use C11,
-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Werror -g -O1 -fsanitize=address,undefined
-fno-omit-frame-pointer. The instrumented app and both the original Gate 4
and twenty-card runners passed their 34 and 31 cases without a reported
sanitizer error. ASAN_OPTIONS=detect_leaks=0 disables leak detection;
UBSAN_OPTIONS=halt_on_error=1 stops on a detected undefined operation.
This is not a leak check. Actual run output: twentyCardSanitizerLog.txt.

MinGW C11 with warnings treated as errors compiled the Windows app, build
helper, Gate 4 runner, twenty-card runner and desktop resize/search runner.
The commands are in gateFourWindowsBuildLog.txt. Cross-compilation does not
prove Windows execution. The old gateFourSanitizerLog.txt records the earlier
five-product version, not a second run of the expanded catalog.
