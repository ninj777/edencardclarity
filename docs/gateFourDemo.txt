Prepare the computer

1. Extract the whole project and build TestSolution.slnx in Visual Studio.
2. Run with --gate4 --gui. The project already has these debugger arguments.
3. Use a throwaway save for practice if needed:
     build\cardClarity.exe --gate4 --gui --wallet-file gateDemoCards.txt
4. Add the five real products. Save, close and reopen with the same arguments.
5. Select each saved card. Scroll through its benefits, restrictions, pricing
   notes, source link and October 30 check date. These are public snapshots.
6. Open Compare a purchase. The examples below use November 2, 2026.
   Amounts entered in the form are dollars, not cents.

The six required purchases

1. Eligible U.S. supermarket, $100, direct. Prior Amex grocery spending $0.
   Savor: 3%, $100 * 0.03 = $3.
   Blue Cash Everyday: 3%, $100 * 0.03 = $3.
   Expected: both tie for the best estimated cashback.
   Actual portable check: both $3.00. See Gate 4 case 1.

2. Dining, $50, direct.
   Savor and Freedom Unlimited: 3%, $50 * 0.03 = $1.50 each.
   Expected: both tie for the best estimate.
   Actual portable check: both $1.50. Cases 2 and 15.

3. Eligible U.S. gas station, $60, direct. Prior Amex gas spending $0.
   Blue Cash Everyday: 3%, $60 * 0.03 = $1.80.
   Expected: Blue Cash Everyday wins.
   Actual portable check: $1.80. Case 3.

4. Drugstore, $40, direct.
   Freedom Unlimited: 3%, $40 * 0.03 = $1.20.
   Expected: Freedom Unlimited wins.
   Actual portable check: $1.20. Case 4.

5. Other eligible purchase, $80, direct.
   Active Cash: 2%, $80 * 0.02 = $1.60.
   Expected: Active Cash wins.

6. Hotel, $200, Capital One Travel.
   Quicksilver and Savor: 5%, $200 * 0.05 = $10 each.

Extra checks for the higher rating

Tie: the dining example must name both Savor and Freedom Unlimited.
Limit: eligible Amex supermarket $30 with $5,990 already spent that calendar
year. $10 * 3% + $20 * 1% = $0.50. The display must explain both portions.
Exhausted limit: use prior spending $6,000; that $30 earns $0.30 on Amex.
Unknown limit: leave prior spending blank; Amex shows $0.30-$0.90 and the
program does not claim a definite winner.
Different portal: hotel $200 through Chase Travel gives Chase $10, while
Quicksilver gives $3 and Savor gives $2.
Date: qualifying Lyft $100 on 2027-09-30 gives Chase $2; on 2027-10-01 the
modeled promotion has ended and the snapshot base estimate is $1.50.
Invalid input: try a negative amount, letters, 1.001 and 2026-02-29, then a
valid answer. No invalid value should become a calculation.

What to explain

The wallet stores stable IDs. The catalog holds names, rates and source notes.
A Purchase struct groups the amount in cents, category, booking service,
date and prior cap spending. RewardEstimate groups the result and whether
it depends on unknown information. Enums name the choices. Functions do the
work; an enum is a type, not a method.

The calculator gets the total eligible rate, splits at a cap if needed,
rounds the complete reward once, and compares the selected real cards.
The console and window call that same code. The old sample calculation is
separate and does not determine a real card's advertised reward.

Benefit notes must stay visible: Amex streaming credit needs enrollment;
Chase's DashPass has an activation deadline and paid renewal. These are not
automatically counted as cash. Source dates do not guarantee later terms.
Unknown pricing is labeled unconfirmed. An annual fee of $0 does not mean
there are no other costs. Estimates exclude interest and transaction fees.
